- Japanese cards carry their right English names and their own TCGplayer products. The name rules read only the mechanics a card prints (ex, GX, VMAX, δ, Star) and keep its owner and kind, so 957 names are put right: "Clefable Clefable" is Clefable, Bruno's Machamp and Erika's Oddish carry their owners, Dark Charizard and Shining Magikarp their kinds, SM12a's Lucario & Melmetal GX its partner, and a trainer is no longer named after a species its name holds (ポケモンファンクラブ is no Krabby). A vintage set's machine-translated printed name (おしっこ under Weezing) is no longer shown beside the English one. A card is linked to a TCGplayer product only where the names agree and to one product only, so SM10's Krabby, Martial Arts Dojo, MC's Exeggcute and neo2's Houndour (U) read their own prices and pictures, and the six cards that held another card's price history lose it. Nine sets TCGplayer titles differently are found by hand (Holon Phantoms, Pokémon Card Web, SM1p and SM2p among them), which links 523 more cards, and the duplicate sm2+ set is gone. TCGdex's swapped records for Fusion Arts 126 to 129 and Storm Emeralda 98 and 99 are read from the right numbers; Kagayaku reads Radiant Rare, a card with no rarity reads None, and ADV5's Dodrio is a Pokémon.
